What good IT looks like at each stage

Most teams do not need an enterprise IT stack. They need the basics done properly. Here is what usually matters as a team grows.

Just me

You and a laptop

  • Business email on your own domain, not a free Gmail address
  • A laptop with disk encryption, automatic updates and a screen lock
  • A password manager and two-step sign-in everywhere
  • Cloud storage plus a second backup copy of your files
  • Safer home Wi-Fi; no firewall appliance needed yet
  • DPDP basics: collect only what you need and keep it protected
2–10 people

Your first team

  • Shared email, calendars and drives with two-step sign-in
  • Company laptops with encryption and basic device management
  • A team password manager with shared vaults
  • A business-grade router or firewall and separate guest Wi-Fi
  • Cloud hosting for your product with sensible access rules
  • Automated backups, with a restore test
  • A joiner-and-leaver checklist so access ends on the last day
  • DPDP basics: limit who sees customer data and log incidents
11–50 people

A growing company

  • Single sign-on and role-based access
  • Managed device policies: lock, wipe and update remotely
  • A firewall with VPN for remote and office staff
  • Separate staging and production environments
  • Central backups and written recovery steps
  • Security monitoring and alerts once you hold customer data
  • A joiner, mover and leaver process with approvals
  • DPDP readiness: a data inventory, retention rules and a breach plan

These are technical safeguards, not legal advice. Not sure which stage you are in? Tell us on a free call.

Honest advice

Enterprise or open source? We help you pick

Neither is better in every case. The right choice depends on your team, your budget and who will look after it. This is how we think about the common decisions.

Common IT decisions: when to choose the enterprise option and when to choose the open-source option
Decision Enterprise option: choose it when… Open-source option: choose it when…
Email, files & collaboration

Google Workspace – your team works in the browser and wants the simplest administration.

Microsoft 365 – you rely on desktop Word, Excel and Outlook, clients send Office files, or you need built-in device and security management.

Nextcloud – your data must stay on servers you control and someone can keep that server updated.
Where your apps run

AWS or Azure – traffic may grow fast or be unpredictable, you have no server room, and pay-as-you-go billing suits you.

Proxmox on your own servers – workloads are steady, you want costs that stay flat, and someone can look after hardware, power and failures.
Firewall

Fortinet – you want a vendor support contract, a polished console and purpose-built hardware, and the licence cost fits your budget.

pfSense – you are comfortable with a hands-on setup and want a flexible firewall and VPN on standard hardware without per-device licences.
Backup

Veeam – you back up many kinds of systems (VMware, Hyper-V, cloud, Microsoft 365) and want broad, vendor-backed restore options.

Proxmox Backup Server – your servers already run on Proxmox. It is built for them, with deduplication and encryption, and no licence fee for the software (paid support is optional).
Security monitoring

Splunk – you need deep analytics across very large log volumes and have the budget and staff to run it.

Wazuh – you want threat detection, file-integrity monitoring and compliance-style checks without per-GB licensing, and someone to manage it for you.
