Nextcloud vs Microsoft 365 for Indian Businesses: How to Choose

This is rarely a simple feature comparison. The better decision usually depends on how much a business values recurring cost control, data ownership, user expectations, and the internal capacity required to support the chosen platform.

Published March 30, 2026 | 6 min read

Microsoft 365 offers a familiar cloud collaboration environment with broad adoption, mature productivity apps, and a polished user experience. Nextcloud, on the other hand, becomes attractive when the business wants stronger control over data location, lower dependency on recurring subscription costs, and a self-hosted collaboration stack that can be shaped around internal needs.

When Microsoft 365 makes sense

If the business depends heavily on the Microsoft ecosystem, needs standardized productivity workflows, and wants vendor-managed convenience, Microsoft 365 is often the easier choice operationally. Adoption is usually faster when users already know the tools.

When Nextcloud becomes attractive

Nextcloud is compelling when data control, self-hosting, predictable infrastructure ownership, and long-term licensing efficiency matter more. It can work especially well for organizations that already operate internal infrastructure or want to build a broader private collaboration environment around file sharing, access control, and controlled integrations.

  • Businesses looking to reduce recurring SaaS spend
  • Teams that need stronger control over where collaboration data lives
  • Organizations building a broader open-source internal stack
  • Environments where privacy and customization matter

The real decision factors

The right choice depends on user behavior, support expectations, storage patterns, mobile needs, compliance requirements, and internal IT capacity. In some cases, the answer is not one or the other. A hybrid approach can make more sense depending on department needs and transition planning.

Which model fits which type of business

Microsoft 365 usually fits best when user familiarity, standardized productivity workflows, and low-friction cloud support are the highest priorities. Nextcloud becomes more attractive when the business wants more control over data location, recurring software spend, and the shape of the collaboration environment itself.

When a hybrid path makes sense

Some organizations do not need to choose one platform for every user. A hybrid model can work well when one group needs strong Microsoft compatibility while another team benefits more from self-hosted control, internal sharing workflows, or lower recurring licensing cost.

Think in total cost, not licence price

Microsoft 365 cost grows with every user you add, and it is easy to predict. Nextcloud shifts the cost from subscriptions to servers, storage, backups and the time of whoever looks after them. That trade can pay off when you have many users or already run your own infrastructure. For a very small team, the admin effort can easily cancel out the saving.

The honest way to compare is to put both on one sheet: licences or subscriptions, hosting, storage growth, backup, monitoring, admin hours and the cost of training people. Security and who is responsible

With Microsoft 365, Microsoft operates the underlying infrastructure, but you still own identity and access: multi-factor sign-in, sharing policies, device rules and who has admin rights. With self-hosted Nextcloud, you own all of that and the server as well: operating system updates, application upgrades, TLS certificates, hardening, backups and restore tests.

A well-maintained self-hosted server can be very secure. A neglected one is a bigger risk than a properly configured cloud tenant. Be realistic about who will do the maintenance before you choose.

What a migration involves

Whichever way you move, plan it in stages rather than as a weekend cutover:

  • Files and folders: usually the simplest part, but folder structures and long file names need checking.
  • Calendars and contacts: can be exported and imported, then verified with a few users first.
  • Email: a separate decision from file sharing; moving it has its own plan and risks.
  • Permissions and sharing links: these rarely carry over cleanly and normally need to be rebuilt.
  • People: a short training session and a pilot group reduce support requests later.

Run the old and new systems side by side for a while, and keep the old one until you have confirmed that everything important has arrived.

Questions to answer before you decide

  • How many people will use it, and how will that number change over the next two years?
  • Does anyone depend on desktop Outlook, Excel macros or other Microsoft-specific features?
  • Do clients send you Office files, and do they expect them back unchanged?
  • Is there a legal, contractual or customer reason to keep data in a specific place?
  • Who would patch, back up and monitor a self-hosted server, and what happens when they are away?
  • What is your budget for hosting and support, not just licences?
  • Would a hybrid setup, with both in use, be easier to adopt than a full switch?

Frequently asked questions: Nextcloud vs Microsoft 365

Is Nextcloud free?

The community edition has no licence fee, but running it is not free: you pay for servers or hosting, storage, backups and administration time. Paid enterprise subscriptions with vendor support are also available.

Can Nextcloud fully replace Microsoft 365?

For file sharing, calendars, contacts and browser-based document editing, often yes. Teams that rely on Exchange email, desktop Office integration or Teams meetings usually keep some Microsoft services, which is why a hybrid approach is common.

Who looks after a self-hosted Nextcloud server?

Someone has to. That means regular updates, backups with restore tests, monitoring and security hardening. It can be your own IT person or an IT partner such as JwithKP.

Can we run Microsoft 365 and Nextcloud together?

Yes. Some businesses keep Microsoft 365 for email and Office apps while using Nextcloud for file sharing or for data that must stay on their own servers.
